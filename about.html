Our Story

We were set a start-up budget of under £10 and told to make it into a real business. This is what we did with it.

The gap

Every "kids'" weight we found was just a small adult one

We went looking for hand weights sized for children and kept finding the same thing: full-size dumbbells in pink or blue, sold as if a smaller colour makes them a kids' product. Bars too wide to grip properly, and nothing built for a kid who's going to drop it, more than once, on purpose.

The budget

Less than £10 to turn that into stock

No investors, no loan beyond the challenge pot, and definitely no factory. Every penny had to go into materials that would actually hold up — fabric, filling, thread — bought as cheaply as we could find it without buying rubbish.

The build

Cutting, filling and sewing the first batch ourselves

The first pairs were made on a kitchen table: cut to size, filled and weighed on a set of kitchen scales so every pair matched, then stitched twice round every seam because one line of stitching wasn't going to survive a nine-year-old. What didn't pass our own drop test didn't go on sale.

Now

Still running on the same rule: spend less than it's worth

Every price on the Range page still has to cover materials, our time, and a bit left over — that was the whole point of the challenge. Nothing here is venture-funded or mass-produced; it's a small batch, made properly, sized for the people actually using it.

The point of it

Strength training shouldn't start with gear built for someone twice your size

Kids who want to get stronger deserve equipment that fits their hands, not a scaled-down version of what an adult would use. That's the whole brief we set ourselves, and it's the only spec that hasn't changed since day one.
